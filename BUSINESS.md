# Launch assumptions and next milestones

The user wants a profitable English/STEP learning product. Profitability has not been validated. The current product is a pilot, with no paying users, validated conversion data or revenue claims.

## Intended offer

Arabic-speaking STEP learners get a daily study loop: try a short exercise, understand errors, rehearse a related sentence with a tutor, revisit vocabulary. The differentiator to validate is useful Arabic feedback connecting exam practice with spoken confidence.

## Proposed packaging

Free: starter exercises, vocabulary and progress. Proposed Plus: SAR 49/month for metered AI tutoring, with browser-assisted voice and eventually a larger adaptive curriculum. This is a pricing hypothesis, not a live purchasable plan. Unlimited audio is not promised: usage should be capped according to measured provider cost.

## Pilot sequence

1. Supply the AI service binding and test real Arabic corrections, grammar guidance and English conversation with a handful of representative learners. Verify spoken-mode compatibility on their actual devices.
2. Expand the small question set with reviewed, original material and track skill-level errors. Do not scrape or copy protected exam content or imply official affiliation.
3. Finish account recovery, email verification, secure production deployment, database backups and privacy/retention controls before inviting public signups.
4. Choose a payment provider that supports the business's Saudi registration and SAR settlement. Implement hosted checkout, signed webhooks, subscription entitlements, refunds and applicable tax requirements. No paid flow exists now.
5. Run a small consent-based pilot; measure activation (first completed practice), repeat learning, conversion, churn and AI cost per active learner before buying significant advertising.

## Unit economics to calculate from real usage

Contribution per subscriber = collected subscription revenue minus payment fees, applicable tax, AI token/audio expense, hosting and variable support cost. Acquisition spend should follow measured retention and contribution, not promises of high income. The SAR 49 figure is only a starting experiment.
